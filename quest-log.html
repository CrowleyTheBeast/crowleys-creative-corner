Quest Log #3 — Apparently I’m Building a Game Studio July 2026

Entry Three. The workshop has acquired more workbenches while I wasn’t looking.

Alright—July got bigger than expected.

I thought I was tending one particular fire: Dimensional Mage, the story that gave this Corner its first real reason to exist. Then I looked around and realized there were game systems on one table, worldbuilding notes on another, community ideas pinned beside the quest board, and at least one metaphorical goblin making off with my organizational skills.

Somewhere along the way, Crowley’s Creative Corner stopped being only a future home for things I might make and became a working creative studio full of things I am actively making. There are stories, games, worlds, and community-building experiments here now. That is exciting. It is also mildly chaotic and a little intimidating, which is probably how you know it is real.

The biggest shift this month was Guck finding its direction. Guck is becoming an idle evolution and resource-management game about helping a strange, flat being understand existence. The path begins with strange matter, moves into particles and elements, then keeps reaching toward molecules, organic systems, and eventually living organisms. Small beginnings. Very normal destination. No existential pressure whatsoever.

More importantly, I figured out what Guck is not. Earlier ideas leaned on health, lives, combat, and treating Dark Matter like a basic enemy. That framing never quite belonged. Guck is not about surviving an attack or defeating darkness. It is about absorbing matter, balancing composition, managing resources, unlocking new stages, and eventually automating eras you have learned to handle. Dark Matter is part of the system, not a monster waiting to be punched.

That clearer direction gives me a much better lantern for the road ahead. The current work is focused on making the Particle Era feel satisfying: understandable resource flow, meaningful upgrades, real progression, automation that feels earned, saving that can be trusted, and support for Android. It is still in active development, but now the pieces are gathering around the same idea instead of politely arguing from opposite ends of the workshop.

Abyss Guardians also became unmistakably its own project. To be completely clear: Abyss Guardians is separate from Guck. It is an original supernatural abyss-defense game about two guardians stationed on opposing sides of an impossible divide. The foundation is being built as a focused single-player experience first.

This one grew through multiple carefully tested stages rather than one enormous leap into the dark. That steady process has carried it to a substantial core milestone, and the current foundation has been confirmed through runtime testing. There is still plenty of abyss beyond the lantern light, but the ground under the project is real, tested, and worth building on carefully.

July also taught me—again, because apparently lessons enjoy repeat visits—that building a game is only part of game development. Testing matters. Documentation matters. Keeping separate projects clearly separated matters. And “I’ll remember what this system does later” is apparently another trap set by Past Me. Past Me remains confident, mysterious, and absolutely unavailable for follow-up questions.

There is something grounding about proving a system works instead of merely feeling like it probably works. There is also something humbling about writing down why it works and discovering that the explanation has wandered away to become folklore. The more projects the Corner holds, the more important those habits become. Every workbench needs labels. Every quest needs a map. Every map should ideally avoid contradicting the person who drew it last month.

So what comes next? I’ll keep improving Guck’s Particle Era. I’ll carefully advance Abyss Guardians from its verified foundation. And I’ll keep shaping the wider creative workshop around them and Dimensional Mage—one honest piece at a time. No grand countdowns carved into the tavern wall. No promises that the next turn in the road will arrive on a perfectly obedient schedule. Just the work, the lessons, and the next useful step.

The Corner feels more alive than it did a month ago. A little louder, too. I am learning how to keep several lanterns lit without setting the quest journal on fire, and I’m proud of what is taking shape here—even when it makes me nervous.

Thanks for sitting by the campfire while the workshop grows. Throw on another log, mind the loose blueprints, and come back for the next entry.

Quest Log #2 — Past Me Has Some Explaining to Do  June 2026

Entry Dos. The campfire is still going babyyyy!!!

Alright — Quest Log number two. Spent the week crawling back through my own vault with a lantern, only to discover the single most important map in the whole thing contradicts itself. Three facts. Can't all be true. And — plot twist — I wrote all three.

So that's going great.

Turns out "I'll fix it later" is just "I'll find it at the worst possible moment" in a nicer coat. Still: locked down a couple of reveals, untangled the timeline, and made my peace with the fact that the biggest ongoing threat to this story is, and has always been, a guy named me.

Map's a little more honest than it was. More honest than me, which — given the bar — isn't the flex it sounds like.

Still building. Still here.

Throwing some more logs on the fire.

Hope I see you at the next campfire. 🕯️ ;)

Quest Log #1 — Foundation  May 2026

Entry One. The campfire is just getting started.

Alright — Quest Log number one. If you're reading this, you found the Corner early, and I appreciate that more than you know. I'd say I'm not nervous about anyone actually reading this, but I'd be lying, and the Quest Log is supposed to be honest.

May 2026. The site is up. The pages exist. And I'm sitting here realizing that "the foundation is being built" is the most accurate thing I've ever written about myself.

The big project right now is my book — a High-Fantasy story that I've been building in my head, in my notes, in scattered files, and finally, properly, in an actual system. I have characters with full sheets. A world with six cities, layers, a magic system built on resonance and emotional truth. A main arc — the Corvin/Cult Arc — that I'm genuinely excited about. Themes about family, loyalty, what people do when the price is right, and what it costs to finally look at where you came from.

The honest progress report? The bones are solid. The architecture is there. Actual scenes? That's what Quest Two is going to be about. (My anxiety said to apologize for that. I told it to sit down.)

This entry is mostly just: I started. The Corner exists. The log is open. Which sounds small until you realize how many times I almost talked myself out of it on the walk over. (Anxiety loves a good "maybe just don't" moment. Very classic of it.)

Some quests start with a battle. Some start with sitting down at the table, lighting a candle, and picking up the journal. This one started here.

See you at the next campfire. 🕯️
