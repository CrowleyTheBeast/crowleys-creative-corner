Quest Log #5 — A Month in the Dark (and Something Big Climbed Out) November 16, 2026

Entry Five. Grab a log, it's a long one. Something has been busy down in the abyss, and so have I.

Last time I sat at this campfire I said Rasps rising from all along the abyss was "still on the drawing board." That was a month ago. It's in. So is a boss, a whole family of new Rasps, eight elements, a helicopter, and a mushroom. Let me walk you through it.

First, an honest note about the browser version. The version you can play here on the Corner is a few weeks behind. It already has the bigger canyon, Rasps surfacing from anywhere along the fog with a little chitter to warn you, zoom on right mouse, Missile Pod salvos, fences and landmines, the new menus, and touch controls for phones and tablets. Everything below that is new is in my desktop build for now. The browser catches up in a future update.

Play Abyss Guardians

Meet the Broodmother. Wave 10 now has a boss. She's enormous, she climbs the cliff toward your partner's tower across the canyon, and she lays egg sacs on the way up. Her shell shrugs off most of what you throw at it, but every so often her abdomen splits open and a glowing weak spot shows itself. That's your moment. She also feeds on what's left of fallen Rasps and hatches nastier ones out of it, so keeping the canyon tidy matters. Take her down and her brood withers with her. She's in solo for now; co-op gets her later.

The Rasps started mixing. Some of them figured out they're stronger together, which is the last thing I wanted them to learn. The Raider is a small Brute riding a Crawling Rasp. The Winged Crawler glides in from the canyon rim. The Spider Brute is exactly as bad as it sounds. The Sky Raider is a Flying Rasp carrying a Brute in its claws, and if you shoot the Flyer down, the Brute falls... and then gets up and keeps coming. The Winged Brute rounds out the family. They show up one by one as you get deeper into a run, and they drop parts from both of their parents.

Speaking of Brutes. They're three times bigger now, with twice the health, and they climb the cliff hand over hand. You'll see them coming. That doesn't make it better.

Eight elements. Rasps can now carry an element: Ember, Frost, Storm, Earth, Grass, Wind, Poison and Radiation. You can tell which by their colour. To deal with them you research five kinds of turret ammo at the bench (Standard, Incendiary, Cryo, Arc and Piercing), load two at a time, and swap between them in the middle of a wave. Your tower look now has an element too, and it gives you a bonus. You can even charge your mines with fire, ice or lightning.

Survival is 60 waves now. Elemental Rasps start trickling in around wave 10 and get more common the deeper you go. Make it to 60 and you win. Endless plays those same 60 waves and then keeps going, with every wave after 60 themed around one element.

The fallen don't stay quiet. Every Rasp you kill leaves a splatter behind. If enough of them die in the same spot, that pile hatches into a brand new Rasp at the start of the next wave. Between waves you can walk over and scrub a pile away before it gets the chance. There's also a new Scavenger upgrade if you want more Rasp parts out of every kill.

A proper first day on the job. New guardians now arrive by helicopter. Someone hands you a headset, you get shoved out the door, and the helicopter leaves you there. From then on a voice in your headset talks you through your first break and your first wave. You can skip it if you've been here before, and there's a new How to Play page on the menu for later.

You have a body now. Pick your guardian from eight: knights, barbarians, mages, rogues, and a few skeletons who refuse to retire. Press C on foot to switch between first and third person, so you can actually watch yourself chop wood, mine stone and pick plants. Your partner across the canyon has a body too, standing right behind their gun.

The tower grew a backyard. There's a garage behind each tower now, with the upgrade bench inside. Get the relay on the roof running and the deck radio wakes up and tells you what's coming next wave. The bench and the defenses table start the match covered in tarps, and you pull the tarp off once you've gathered enough to build. Reach wave 10 in Survival and you earn a greenhouse behind the garage.

Healgui. A glowing little mushroom grows around the canyon now. Eat one raw for a quick ten-second boost when things go wrong, or take two to the bench and brew a proper Health Serum. Hasteweed brews into a Stamina Serum, which you'll want, because there's a stamina bar now and sprinting isn't free anymore.

It looks and sounds like the abyss now. Waves happen at night, and the breaks between them glow amber like dusk. There's music when the Rasps come and a quiet canyon soundscape when they don't, with volume sliders in the pause menu. Past the mountains, a thick wall of fog swallows the edge of the world, so no more peeking off the end of the map.

And a pile of smaller things. Falling off something tall hurts now, and falling off something really tall ends your run, so use the ladder. The ladder also works properly at the top and bottom, which it very much did not before. There are achievements, and clearing waves 10 and 20 in Survival lets you start later runs from there. The first rank of every upgrade is cheaper, the later ranks cost more. And there's a Field Guide on the menu if you want to read up on what's trying to eat you.

Coming next: real farming in the greenhouse, where scrubbed Rasp remains turn into mulch for your crops (the Rasps are fertilizer now, which feels fair), and a trophy room at the bottom of your tower to show off what you've taken down. Both are on the workbench, so no promises on dates.

If something breaks, feels unfair, or is secretly way too easy, leave me a comment on the play page. I read every one.

Thanks for sitting by the campfire. Now go keep the canyon tidy. Something big is watching it. 🕯️

Quest Log #4 — The Abyss Has a Front Door Now October 15, 2026

Entry Four. Pull up a seat at the edge of the abyss. You can play now.

Alright, big one. Abyss Guardians: Low Poly is playable in your browser, right here on the Corner. No download, no installer. Click the button, hold the line.

Play Abyss Guardians

Quick refresher: you're a guardian on a tower at the edge of a canyon, manning a mounted turret while waves of Rasps climb up out of the fog. Your partner holds the tower across the divide. The turret runs hot, so you can't just hold the trigger and pray. Managing heat is half the fight.

Here's what changed today.

Abyss Shards are back. Glowing crystals now grow on the rock shelf at the edge of the abyss. Harvest them and you can push your upgrades past their old limit, all the way to rank 5. There are only a handful of them each match, so where you spend them actually matters.

The upgrade bench got a makeover. The old menu was a wall of text. Now every upgrade is its own card that shows what it does, the bonus you get, and what it costs. You can finally see your bonuses without doing math in your head mid-panic.

The Rasps got less predictable. They used to come up the cliff in a fairly tidy order. Now they pick different spots to climb, drift sideways on the way up, and show up with a bit more randomness in their timing. Same number of them per wave, they're just harder to read. Rude, honestly, but that's the point.

A practice mode, for me. The desktop build now has a developer mode so I can test any wave whenever I want: skip waves, hand myself materials, make the towers unkillable. It's not in the browser version. You have to earn your wins the honest way.

The browser version fits your screen. The first web build cropped the menus unless you went fullscreen. Now the game scales to whatever size your window is, and there's a new Full window button that fills the browser tab without going fully fullscreen.

All of this sits on top of a busy few days before it: nine Story waves that build from Crawling Rasps to Flying Rasps to Brute Rasps, an Endless mode for anyone who wants to see how deep the dark goes, and a whole upgrade system. Rasps drop parts when they die, the canyon has wood, stone and glowing Hasteweed to gather between waves, and the bench turns all of it into better guns, better cooling, tougher armour and faster repairs.

Coming next: right now the Rasps come up from a few familiar places. The plan is for them to rise out of the fog from all along the abyss, with a sound and a glow to warn you just before they surface. That one is still on the drawing board, so no promises on when.

A fair warning: the browser version is a little lighter on the fancy lighting than the desktop one, and it's a big first download, so give it a moment to load. If something breaks, feels unfair, or is secretly way too easy, I want to hear about it.

Thanks for sitting by the campfire. Now go stand on the tower. 🕯️

Quest Log #3 — Apparently I’m Building a Game Studio July 2026

Entry Three. The workshop has acquired more workbenches while I wasn’t looking.

Alright—July got bigger than expected.

I thought I was tending one particular fire: Dimensional Mage, the story that gave this Corner its first real reason to exist. Then I looked around and realized there were game systems on one table, worldbuilding notes on another, community ideas pinned beside the quest board, and at least one metaphorical goblin making off with my organizational skills.

Somewhere along the way, Crowley’s Creative Corner stopped being only a future home for things I might make and became a working creative studio full of things I am actively making. There are stories, games, worlds, and community-building experiments here now. That is exciting. It is also mildly chaotic and a little intimidating, which is probably how you know it is real.

The biggest shift this month was Guck finding its direction. Guck is becoming an idle evolution and resource-management game about helping a strange, flat being understand existence. The path begins with strange matter, moves into particles and elements, then keeps reaching toward molecules, organic systems, and eventually living organisms. Small beginnings. Very normal destination. No existential pressure whatsoever.

More importantly, I figured out what Guck is not. Earlier ideas leaned on health, lives, combat, and treating Dark Matter like a basic enemy. That framing never quite belonged. Guck is not about surviving an attack or defeating darkness. It is about absorbing matter, balancing composition, managing resources, unlocking new stages, and eventually automating eras you have learned to handle. Dark Matter is part of the system, not a monster waiting to be punched.

That clearer direction gives me a much better lantern for the road ahead. The current work is focused on making the Particle Era feel satisfying: understandable resource flow, meaningful upgrades, real progression, automation that feels earned, saving that can be trusted, and support for Android. It is still in active development, but now the pieces are gathering around the same idea instead of politely arguing from opposite ends of the workshop.

Abyss Guardians also became unmistakably its own project. To be completely clear: Abyss Guardians is separate from Guck. It is an original supernatural abyss-defense game about two guardians stationed on opposing sides of an impossible divide. The foundation is being built as a focused single-player experience first.

This one grew through multiple carefully tested stages rather than one enormous leap into the dark. That steady process has carried it to a substantial core milestone, and the current foundation has been confirmed through runtime testing. There is still plenty of abyss beyond the lantern light, but the ground under the project is real, tested, and worth building on carefully.

July also taught me—again, because apparently lessons enjoy repeat visits—that building a game is only part of game development. Testing matters. Documentation matters. Keeping separate projects clearly separated matters. And “I’ll remember what this system does later” is apparently another trap set by Past Me. Past Me remains confident, mysterious, and absolutely unavailable for follow-up questions.

There is something grounding about proving a system works instead of merely feeling like it probably works. There is also something humbling about writing down why it works and discovering that the explanation has wandered away to become folklore. The more projects the Corner holds, the more important those habits become. Every workbench needs labels. Every quest needs a map. Every map should ideally avoid contradicting the person who drew it last month.

So what comes next? I’ll keep improving Guck’s Particle Era. I’ll carefully advance Abyss Guardians from its verified foundation. And I’ll keep shaping the wider creative workshop around them and Dimensional Mage—one honest piece at a time. No grand countdowns carved into the tavern wall. No promises that the next turn in the road will arrive on a perfectly obedient schedule. Just the work, the lessons, and the next useful step.

The Corner feels more alive than it did a month ago. A little louder, too. I am learning how to keep several lanterns lit without setting the quest journal on fire, and I’m proud of what is taking shape here—even when it makes me nervous.

Thanks for sitting by the campfire while the workshop grows. Throw on another log, mind the loose blueprints, and come back for the next entry.

Quest Log #2 — Past Me Has Some Explaining to Do  June 2026

Entry Dos. The campfire is still going babyyyy!!!

Alright — Quest Log number two. Spent the week crawling back through my own vault with a lantern, only to discover the single most important map in the whole thing contradicts itself. Three facts. Can't all be true. And — plot twist — I wrote all three.

So that's going great.

Still building. Still here.

Throwing some more logs on the fire.

Quest Log #1 — Foundation  May 2026

Entry One. The campfire is just getting started.

Alright — Quest Log number one. If you're reading this, you found the Corner early, and I appreciate that more than you know. I'd say I'm not nervous about anyone actually reading this, but I'd be lying, and the Quest Log is supposed to be honest.

May 2026. The site is up. The pages exist. And I'm sitting here realizing that "the foundation is being built" is the most accurate thing I've ever written about myself.

The big project right now is my book — a High-Fantasy story that I've been building in my head, in my notes, in scattered files, and finally, properly, in an actual system. I have characters with full sheets. A world with six cities, layers, a magic system built on resonance and emotional truth. A main arc — the Corvin/Cult Arc — that I'm genuinely excited about. Themes about family, loyalty, what people do when the price is right, and what it costs to finally look at where you came from.

The honest progress report? The bones are solid. The architecture is there. Actual scenes? That's what Quest Two is going to be about. (My anxiety said to apologize for that. I told it to sit down.)

This entry is mostly just: I started. The Corner exists. The log is open. Which sounds small until you realize how many times I almost talked myself out of it on the walk over. (Anxiety loves a good "maybe just don't" moment. Very classic of it.)

Some quests start with a battle. Some start with sitting down at the table, lighting a candle, and picking up the journal. This one started here.

See you at the next campfire. 🕯️
